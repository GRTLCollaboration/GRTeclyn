# Findings

Everything below is reproducible from `data/` with the scripts in `analysis/`.
Numbers are quoted in code units (G = c = 1, scalar mass m = 1); drifts are
`x(t) − x(0)` along the pair axis.

---

## 1. The experiment and its falsifiable matrix

Bondi (1957): a pair made of one positive-active-mass and one
negative-active-mass body self-accelerates. The negative-mass body is
*attracted* by the positive one; the positive body is *repelled* by the
negative one; both accelerations point the same way, and total momentum stays
zero because the negative body carries negative momentum. The configuration
has never been evolved in full 3+1 numerical relativity with dynamical,
constraint-solved matter.

The bicomplex scalar model supplies exactly the required sign structure
(details in [`MATTER_MODEL.md`](MATTER_MODEL.md)): two complex scalar fields
obeying the **same** Klein–Gordon equation — so both have positive inertial and
passive gravitational mass — with the sign flip living **only** in the Einstein
source, giving the phantom sector negative active mass.

Each cell is a falsifiable prediction, and all five were run:

| cell | prediction | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| `pair_pm` (+,−) | runs away along +x, phantom chasing canonical | **confirmed** |
| `pair_pp` (+,+) | attracts; no net drift of the pair centre | confirmed (null) |
| `pair_mm` (−,−) | mutually repels; no net drift of the pair centre | confirmed (null) |
| `single_p` / `single_m` | drift nowhere; calibrate the per-sector noise floor | confirmed (null) |

## 2. The dressed stars

Both sectors are gravitationally dressed solitons solved at **fixed
frequency** at the ultraweak rung (λ = 10240, μ = 21845333, λ²/μ = 4.8, so
ω_min = 0.316; ω = 0.55 is 45 % binding). The phantom star is solved with the
gravity sign reversed in the metric ODEs only — its self-gravity is repulsive,
so it is puffier, carries negative ADM mass, and its lapse rises above 1 in the
core where the canonical star's dips below.

Family scan (`stars/star_family.csv`, regenerated by
`analysis/star_family_scan.py`):

| 0.53 | 0.019619 | +0.079391 | 0.9745 | 0.019502 | −0.099320 | 1.0286 | 1.251 |

Three structural facts fall out:

1. **The two sectors are not mirror images.** At equal frequency the phantom
   star is always the heavier one in magnitude (20 % at ω = 0.55), because
   repulsive self-gravity puffs it out. The asymmetry shrinks as the stars get
   lighter (4 % at ω = 0.80) — it is a gravitational effect, vanishing in the
   weak-field limit.
2. **The mass curves cross**, which is what makes an equal-|ADM| pair
   possible: the phantom star at **ω = 0.56598** carries ADM = −0.063950
   against the canonical star's +0.063951 at ω = 0.550 — matched to 1 part in
   10⁵.
3. **The branch ends near ω ≈ 0.52–0.53.** Below that no dressed star exists at
   this rung; this is the same obstruction that made the whole first phase of
   the campaign fail (see [`DEBUGGING.md`](DEBUGGING.md)).

Profile tables for the three stars actually evolved are in `stars/` as
`r φ₀(r) α(r)`.

## 4. The runaway

`pair_pm`: canonical star seeded at x = +4, phantom at x = −4, both at rest,
separation 8, no boosts, no pump, pure self-gravity.

| t | x_canon | x_phantom | drift canon | drift phantom | separation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 36.00 | 28.00 | +0.000 | +0.000 | 8.00 |
| 20 | 36.14 | 28.30 | +0.135 | +0.296 | 7.84 |
| 30 | 36.59 | 28.96 | +0.591 | +0.957 | 7.63 |
| 40 | 37.39 | 30.52 | +1.383 | +2.517 | 6.87 |
| 60 | 39.35 | 37.57 | +3.348 | +9.561 | 1.79 |

**Both barycentres move in the same direction (+x), with nothing pushing
them** — the Bondi signature. The controls, run under identical numerics, do
not: `pair_pp` ends at +0.157, `pair_mm` at +0.039, and the single stars at
+0.061 / +0.065, which sets the diagnostic noise floor. The mixed cell exceeds
that floor by a factor of 50–150.

## 5. Quantitative tests against a point-mass reference

`analysis/newtonian_reference.py` integrates the same configuration as two
point masses using the *measured* ADM masses, with the model's own sign
structure (acceleration set by the other body's active mass, independent of the
body's own mass). Comparison at matched times:

| t | drift canon NR / point-mass | drift phantom NR / point-mass | separation NR / point-mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | +0.014 / +0.060 | +0.054 / +0.050 | 7.96 / 8.01 |
| 20 | +0.135 / +0.240 | +0.296 / +0.200 | 7.84 / 8.04 |
| 30 | +0.591 / +0.539 | +0.957 / +0.448 | 7.63 / 8.09 |
| 40 | +1.383 / +0.956 | +2.517 / +0.794 | 6.87 / 8.16 |
| 60 | +3.348 / +2.133 | +9.561 / +1.772 | 1.79 / 8.36 |

### 5.1 The canonical star's push scales with the phantom's active mass — to 0.6 %

This is the cleanest gravitational signature in the campaign, because it
compares the **same diagnostic across two runs that differ in exactly one
quantity**: the phantom star's mass (−0.07696 → −0.06395, a factor 0.831).
Systematic biases in the barycentre measure cancel in the ratio.

| t | canonical drift ratio, eqm/pm (NR) | same ratio, point-mass | pure mass ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | **0.836** | 0.832 | 0.831 |
| 30 | 0.859 | 0.834 | 0.831 |
| 40 | 0.876 | 0.837 | 0.831 |
| 60 | 0.879 | 0.843 | 0.831 |

At t = 20 the measured ratio matches the mass ratio to **0.6 %**. Lighten the
negative mass by 17 %, and the positive star is pushed 16 % less far. The
co-drift is gravitational, and it responds quantitatively to the source.

### 5.2 The phantom's fall depends only on the canonical star — and is reproducible

Between `pair_pm` and `pair_pm_eqm` the canonical source is unchanged, and the
phantom's drift is unchanged with it: ratio **1.002** at t = 20 and 1.002 at
t = 60 (1.02–1.03 in between). Two independent constraint solves and
evolutions, differing in the phantom's own mass by 17 %, reproduce its
trajectory at the sub-percent level. This is both a physics statement (the
phantom's acceleration is set by the *other* body's active mass, as the model
requires) and a strong reproducibility check on the diagnostic.

### 5.3 Where the runs depart from point masses — and why

Two departures, both traceable to the same cause:

- **The phantom overshoots** the point-mass prediction, and increasingly so:
  1.5× at t = 20, 2.1× at t = 30, 3.2× at t = 40, 5.4× at t = 60.
- **The gap closes** (8.00 → 1.79) where the point-mass model says it should
  *grow* slightly (8.00 → 8.36), because |M₋| > |M₊| means the canonical body
  is pushed harder than the phantom is pulled.

The equal-mass run isolates the cause. With |ADM| matched, the point-mass model
predicts **identical drifts and an exactly constant gap** (8.000 at every time).
The NR run instead gives +2.94 / +9.58 and a gap closing to 1.36. Since the
masses are now equal to 1 part in 10⁵, the asymmetry cannot be a mass-ratio
effect.

What remains is geometry: at separation 8 these stars have rms radius ≈ 5, so
**they overlap from t = 0** — the phantom's envelope sits well inside the
canonical star's near field, where the effective pull greatly exceeds ADM/r².
These are extended bodies in contact, not point masses. A constant-gap runaway
requires point-like separation; at this separation the pair closes to
near-contact by t ≈ 60 regardless of mass matching.

### 5.4 Caveat: the late-time magnitudes are contaminated

The barycentre integrates the whole sector, core plus shed radiation. Two
numbers bound how far that can be trusted:

| quantity (`pair_pm`) | t = 0 | t = 20 | t = 60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| total canonical activity | 15.89 | 16.02 | 10.44 |
| rms radius, canonical | 5.05 | 6.00 | 11.54 |

By t = 60 roughly a third of the tracked field has left the accounting region
and the distribution has doubled in extent, so the late-time barycentre is a
mix of a moving core and a spreading halo. **The clean quantitative window is
t ≲ 30**, where totals are stable to ≲ 7 % — and it is exactly there that the
mass-scaling test (§5.1) and the reproducibility test (§5.2) are strongest.
The qualitative result (co-drift, its direction, its exclusivity to the mixed
cell) holds across the whole run; the late-time drift magnitudes should be read
as upper bounds on core displacement, not as precision measurements.

Recommended follow-up: track a core-weighted centroid (e.g. weight by activity²
or fit the peak) alongside the barycentre, so core motion and halo motion can be
separated at late times.

## 6. The phantom sector is the *stable* one

Counter to the naive expectation that antigravitating matter should fly apart,
the phantom star was the best-behaved object in the campaign.

| metric (single stars, t = 0 → 40) | canonical | phantom |
|---|---|---|
| minimum χ (geometric grip) | 0.9894 → 0.8510 (steady slide) | 1.0000 → 1.0011 (**flat**) |
| rms radius (radiation bath) | 5.045 → 12.251 | 5.427 → 6.251 |
| peak-amplitude breathing | ≈ ±8 % | ≈ ±3 % |

The reason is that gravity is not the binding agent here — the scalar
self-interaction is, and it is identical in both sectors. Gravity only dresses
the soliton. Its sign therefore shows up as a *correction*, and the reversed
sign is the stabilising one:

- Around the canonical star, shed field is pulled back and deepens the well, so
  perturbations feed on themselves; around the phantom star, shed field pushes
  itself apart before it can clump.
- The canonical star's exhaust gravitates onto the star (χ slides 0.99 → 0.85);
  the phantom's exhaust anti-gravitates and self-dilutes (χ stays at 1.0009 ±
  0.0002 for the entire run) — a roughly 4× slower bath growth.

The same asymmetry appears in the controls: `pair_mm` (two phantoms) keeps
χ ≈ 1.00 throughout while its material spreads, whereas `pair_pp` (two
canonicals) drives χ down to 0.44 as the pair merges.

## 7. Numerical caveats and their scope

| effect | evidence | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Breathing is resolution-intrinsic** | a re-solve with 30× tighter residuals (Mom 0.64 % → 0.018 %) reproduced the ±8 % breath point-for-point | cosmetic; a resolution notch is the lever, not a tighter solve |
| **The radiation bath cannot leave** | massive-field radiation against massless-wave (Sommerfeld) boundaries; χ drifts down monotonically in canonical cells | mixed cells capped at t = 60; a sponge layer would be needed for longer runs |
| **Barycentre includes the halo** | total activity −35 %, rms ×2.3 by t = 60 | §5.4 — quote t ≲ 30 for precision |
| **No horizon in the measured window** | θ₊ stays positive and no apparent horizon is found in every cell stopped at t = 60; the lapse only dips to ≈ 0.55 | the runaway is not a collapse artefact |
| **Collapse after contact** | the two t = 90 double-box cells form a trapped surface at t = 68.5 (mixed) and t = 69.5 (control) | end state is black-hole formation; measured at one cell size only, not convergence-tested |
| **Single-rank only** | MPI is unusable on this node | performance only; no physics impact |

## 8. What is established, and what is not

**Established.**
1. A gravitationally dressed phantom (negative-active-mass) soliton can be
   constructed as constraint-satisfying initial data and evolved stably — as
   far as we know, the first time this has been done.
2. Released at rest beside a canonical soliton, the pair self-accelerates: both
   barycentres move the same way, at 50–150× the diagnostic noise floor.
3. The effect is exclusive to the mixed pair — both same-sector controls are
   null under identical numerics.
4. The push on the canonical star scales with the phantom's active mass as
   gravity requires (0.6 % agreement), and the phantom's response to an
   unchanged source reproduces across independent runs to 0.2 %.
5. The drift is resolution-independent and box-independent. A uniform-grid
   ladder at cell sizes 0.50 / 0.33 / 0.25 agrees to 2.2 % at t = 15 and 0.5 %
   at t = 60, while the same ladder for the control shows its residual drift
   halving at each refinement — the effect converges, the artefact vanishes
   (`campaign/`, `analysis/convergence_check.md`).
6. The runaway does not need a mass difference: with |ADM| matched between the
   sectors the pair still runs away, 3 % weaker — a resolved difference against
   a 0.3–0.5 % grid spread.
7. The pair radiates a converged gravitational-wave signal. The l = 2, m = 0
   amplitude agrees across four extraction shells in retarded time over
   u = 10–25, to 0.3 % across the resolution ladder and 0.1 % between box
   sizes (`analysis/wave_check.md`). The control has no usable wave window at
   all — its shells never agree — so no control amplitude is quotable.
8. Nothing collapses while the runaway is being measured: no apparent horizon
   and θ₊ > 0 in every cell that stops at t = 60.

**Not established.**
1. *A constant-gap runaway.* At separation 8 the stars overlap and the pair
   closes to near-contact. The clean test needs separation 12–16, or more
   compact stars (both sectors at higher ω, where the family scan shows the
   mass asymmetry also shrinks to a few percent).
2. *Precision drift magnitudes at late times.* See §5.4.
3. *The end state — now observed, not yet measured.* The two double-box cells
   were carried to t = 90, past contact: both form a black hole, the mixed
   pair at t = 68.5 (apparent-horizon radius ≈ 2.5) and the two-positive
   control at t = 69.5 (≈ 4.5), with the lapse collapsing to 0.06 and 0.01 by
   t = 80. A canonical–phantom contact therefore ends in collapse rather than
   in mutual cancellation. This rests on one cell size (0.50) in one box with
   no convergence ladder behind it, and the sector barycentres are meaningless
   once the lumps merge, so no quantitative claim is attached to it yet.
