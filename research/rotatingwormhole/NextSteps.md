Why the signal is matter-driven — and why that's forced by your setup

Your initial data has |Φ|² exactly axisymmetric by construction; all the angular momentum lives in the phase winding, not in any moving density feature. GWs couple to time-varying mass and current multipoles, and for an axisymmetric, equatorially symmetric configuration the nonvanishing moments are the even mass multipoles (M₀, M₂, M₄…) and the odd current multipoles (S₁, S₃…). When the torus collapses axisymmetrically:

The changing mass quadrupole M₂ radiates ℓ=2, m=0, even parity — exactly the burst you see.
The current dipole S₁ = J_z is conserved and never radiates.
The current quadrupole S₂ vanishes by equatorial symmetry — which means your measured Im Ψ₄^{2,0}/Re Ψ₄^{2,0} ≲ 1.2×10⁻³ is not evidence that "rotation is weak"; it's enforced by symmetry. Your null test is actually confirming equatorial symmetry, not measuring rotational radiation.
The first channel where axisymmetric rotation is allowed to radiate is the current octupole S₃ → ℓ=3, m=0, odd parity, sourced by radial redistribution of angular momentum during collapse.

So "the GW is caused by the matter, not the rotation" isn't a bug you can patch in this configuration — it's close to a theorem given axisymmetry. To make rotation radiate you must either look in the channel where it's permitted (ℓ=3 m=0), or break axisymmetry in the density.

Three tiers of fix, cheapest first

Tier 1 — mine the existing runs (no new GPU time). Extract Ψ₄^{3,0} and its parity content from the stored plotfiles. If there's a coherent odd-parity ℓ=3 m=0 burst tracking the collapse, that is the rotational radiation of this spacetime, and you can quote its energy relative to the m=0 quadrupole. Second, compute the angular-momentum flux: dJ_z/dt in GWs is proportional to m mode-by-mode, so m=0 radiation carries exactly zero J_z. You can then make a sharp quantitative statement the paper currently only gestures at: the burst radiates energy but essentially no angular momentum; the remnant retains ≈100% of J_ADM. Third, run a flat-space quadrupole-formula estimate from the matter density history and compare morphology with the extracted burst — that proves the "matter quadrupole" attribution positively instead of by elimination. And note the connection to your rebound: if the collapse can't shed J, the phantom bounce may be partly a centrifugal/angular-momentum bounce, which is testable in Tier 2.

Tier 2 — a spin ladder instead of the broken non-rotating control. Your non-rotating control was pathological for a physical reason worth stating in the paper: a toroidal scalar density without winding is not close to any equilibrium — the m²/ρ² term in the field equation is what supports an off-axis amplitude peak, so removing the winding while keeping the torus shape hands the evolution a violently out-of-equilibrium state. A same-shape zero-spin control is ill-posed in this matter model; rotation and toroidal geometry are inseparable. The honest replacements are differential controls: (a) an m=2 winding torus at matched M_ADM (same topology, ~2× J), (b) an ω ladder within m=1 (your κ-family driver already does amplitude families; a small ω family is the same machinery), and optionally (c) a matched-mass spherical m=0 Q-ball under the same support-cut protocol as the "no rotation, no torus" baseline. If the waveform, collapse depth, and rebound radius shift systematically with J across the ladder, you've isolated rotation's dynamical role even though it doesn't dominate the radiation; if they don't shift, that's a clean negative worth publishing as such.

Tier 3 — a genuinely non-axisymmetric campaign (this is your PRDM2 plan, and it's the right follow-up paper, not a patch to this one). To get radiation caused by rotation you need a rotating m≠0 density pattern: seed an m=2 perturbation on the torus, or use two orbiting compact tori/Q-balls. The literature gives you the mechanism for free — spinning scalar stars are generically unstable to non-axisymmetric modes (Sanchis-Gual/Di Giovanni 2020), with m=1 quenchable by repulsive self-interaction in certain windows (Siemonsen & East 2021) — but note the growth timescales are typically hundreds to thousands of M. Your run is 40 time units and the support cut comes at t=8–10, so spontaneous bar-mode growth will never appear on your window; you must seed it at finite amplitude. A seeded m=2 collapse radiating at ~2Ω_pattern in the (2,2) mode is the "rotation causes the signal" result, and it's a separate paper.
