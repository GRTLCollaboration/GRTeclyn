Overall assessment
The paper is honest about being an engineered configuration and the writing is unusually transparent about failure modes (the "decision chain" section is genuinely good). However, in its current state the central claims are not yet supported at the level asserted in the abstract. There are three internal inconsistencies, one likely mis-attribution of the GW source, one missing definition (the mass M itself), and no convergence study. A referee at PRD would return this as "major revision" on those grounds alone.

Critical issues (blocking)
1. The trapped surface exists before the trigger — undermining "collapse-on-command"
The paper states the trapped surface appears "first at $t\approx2.3,M$" — but the support ramp starts at $t=8$. If a trapped surface exists at $t=2.3$ while the pump holds "quasi-equilibrium", then either (a) the system was already collapsing before the command, or (b) the $\theta_+$ coordinate-sphere proxy is unreliable. There is a third, likely explanation you must address explicitly: a wormhole throat is itself a minimal surface with $\theta_+\approx0$ — your proxy may be flagging the throat, not a genuinely trapped region. Without disentangling this, the "collapse-on-command" headline is not demonstrated. You need:

a pump-on control run (no ramp, identical everything, to $t=40$) showing no collapse and no burst;
a run with a shifted ramp (e.g. $t\in[16,18]$) showing the collapse time shifts accordingly. This pair is the actual proof of causality, and it's cheap.
2. The reported mode is $\ell=2,m=0$ — which does not demonstrate that rotation sources the radiation
The paper's thesis is "rotation supplies the quadrupole, no kick needed." But $m=0$ is the axisymmetric mode. An axisymmetric collapsing torus (rotating or not) sources $\ell=2,m=0$ through its shape alone. The smoking gun for rotation-sourced radiation is power in $m=\pm1$ (from the $m=1$ matter winding) or $m=\pm2$. Your own pipeline extracts all five $m\in{-2..2}$ — report them. Additionally, run the non-rotating control: the same torus profile with $\omega=0, m=0$ plus the identical ramp. If it produces a comparable $\ell=2,m=0$ burst, the correct claim becomes "the toroidal geometry supplies the quadrupole", which is a different (still publishable) result. Right now the causal attribution in the title/abstract is unproven.

3. The mass $M$ is never defined
Every time axis, the $\tau\approx28.6,M$ lifetime, the 169 Hz / 5 Hz detector claims — all are quoted "in units of $M$", but the paper never computes the ADM mass (or any mass) of the spacetime. In code units, what is $M$? Until you measure $M_{\rm ADM}$ (and $J_{\rm ADM}$), all physical-unit scalings in the abstract, Fig. 6f, and the Discussion are unfounded. This is a one-integral fix and it's mandatory.

4. Abstract vs. results vs. logs — internal inconsistencies
Abstract: "the conserved Noether charge is retained, not radiated." Trust section (and the OrbitalPumpPlan log): $Q_{\rm total}$ drifts ~116% and changes sign; $J_z$ drifts ~141%. Retention of $Q_{\rm sphere}$ while $Q_{\rm total}$ changes sign is not "charge retained" — it may be pump-injected charge of opposite sign. The abstract must not state a conservation claim the caveats retract. Decompose the budget (pump injection integral, the pump_work/charge-injection diagnostic you already have, vs. flux through spheres) before claiming anything.
Abstract: lapse "falls from 1 toward $10^{-3}$"; Results first quote 0.033, then $10^{-3}$ at $t\approx30$. Pick one consistent narrative.
"propagating at exactly $v=1.00,c$" — with lags of 6 and 12 $M$ read off a burst ~10 $M$ wide at a finite sampling cadence, "exactly 1.00" is overclaimed. Quote $v = 1.00 \pm \sigma$ with the cross-correlation uncertainty.
5. The extraction radii are entirely in the near zone
You state dominant power at $f\lesssim0.1,M^{-1}$, i.e. wavelength $\lambda\gtrsim60,M$ — while extracting at $R_{\rm ext}=12$–$24$, with the horizon proxy reaching $r\approx9.2$ (nearly touching $R=12$) and the sponge starting at $r=28$. Every extraction sphere is at $R<\lambda/2$, sandwiched between the source and the sponge. The $v=c$ test mitigates but does not cure this. Minimum fixes: (i) extrapolate $r\Psi_4$ to $r\to\infty$ using the standard $1/r$ polynomial fit across your three radii and show it converges; (ii) rerun with a larger box ($L=128$ or 256 via AMR, extraction at 50–100). State plainly in the paper that the current radii are near-zone.

6. No convergence study
Single resolution, with the most dramatic physics (the pinch to $\chi=0.019$ and the "phantom bounce") occurring exactly where gradients are steepest. The bounce in particular could be an under-resolution or 1+log gauge artifact — collapsing-lapse spacetimes are notorious for gauge dynamics mimicking physical rebound. A 3-point resolution ladder (e.g. $dx=0.67/0.5/0.375$, matched ID per your L2 rule) with (a) constraint convergence order, (b) waveform overlap, (c) bounce reproducibility, is the minimum for the bounce claim to survive. Until then, demote the "phantom bounce" from the abstract to "suggestive".

Significant (non-blocking) issues
Constraint norms lack a denominator. $L_2({\rm Ham})\le3.3\times10^{-2}$ — relative to what? Report the relative violation (normalized by the L2 of the individual constraint terms) and compare against the $t=0$ solver residual (~1%). A volume-weighted L2 can also hide a localized blow-up at the throat under a large healthy volume — show the L∞ or an $r<5$ restricted norm too. Also report the CCZ4 $\Theta$/$Z_i$ norms: constraint damping can absorb the pump's Bianchi violation and make the Ham/Mom norms look deceptively flat.
The "no kick" framing is rhetorically overstated. The ramp itself is a time-dependent, spatially localized modification of the field equations — it is the perturbation. What you've removed is an imposed multipolar seed, not perturbation per se. Rephrase: "no seeded multipole; the only external action is the smooth, spherically-structured removal of support."
Horizon proxy on a rotating spacetime. A coordinate-sphere $\theta_+$ with that formula implicitly assumes near-spherical, near-conformally-flat slices; a spinning trapped surface is not a coordinate sphere. Run a genuine AH finder (even post-hoc on checkpoints); then you also get $M_{\rm irr}$ and the horizon spin from the isolated-horizon formula — which would massively strengthen the "final state" section.
No energy budget. Compute $E_{\rm GW}$ from $\Psi_4$, the total pump work $\int$pump_work$,dt$, and $M_{\rm ADM}(t=0)$. Even a rough statement "$E_{\rm GW}\sim10^{-4}M$, pump injected $\sim x,M$" is essential for the LIGO-band claims — strain amplitude, not just frequency, decides detectability, and Fig. 6f presumably assumed something for the distance/amplitude.
Bibliography is thin for the claims made. Missing at minimum: Kleihaus–Kunz rotating phantom wormholes (PRD 90, 121503 is cited, but also their 2014–2016 follow-ups), Volkov–Wöhnert and Radu–Volkov on spinning Q-balls (your eigenstate construction descends directly from these), Torii–Shinkai wormhole dynamics, González–Guzmán–Sarbach is there but the charged/rotating stability literature is not, and any prior work on wormhole GW signatures (e.g. echoes literature) to position the burst claim. Also shirokov_wh_dynamics cited as "companion manuscript, this repository" is not citable — put it on arXiv first and cite the preprint number.
Reproducibility. "Maintained in the project repository" with no URL/DOI. Archive the run params, .gridinit, psi4 series, and analysis scripts on Zenodo and cite the DOI.
