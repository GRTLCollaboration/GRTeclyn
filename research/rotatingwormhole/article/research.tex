\begin{abstract}
We present a 3D numerical-relativity evolution, performed with \texttt{GRTeclyn},
of a \emph{rotating}, actively supported wormhole throat driven to gravitational
collapse ``on command.'' The throat is threaded by an exotic (null-energy-condition
violating) complex scalar field configured as a rotating $m{=}1$ Q-ball torus, a
genuine stationary eigenstate produced by the \texttt{GRTresna} elliptic
initial-data solver. Because a passively twisted rotating lump is not a true
eigenstate and disperses, the throat is held open by a proportional--derivative
(PD) ``matter pump''---an engineered source that supplies the missing stationarity.
Collapse is triggered not by any imposed perturbation but by ramping the support
pump smoothly to zero over the interval $t\in[8,10]\,M$, the rotating analogue of
the static Ellis--Bronnikov $S_{\rm support}\!\to\!0$ collapse branch. With
\emph{zero} imposed quadrupolar kick ($A_\phi=0$), the throat collapses: the lapse
falls from $1$ toward $10^{-3}$, the conformal factor $\chi$ pinches to $0.019$, and a
trapped surface forms ($r_{\rm AH}\!\approx\!9.2$, $\theta_+\!<\!0$) that captures the
exotic matter (the conserved Noether charge is retained, not radiated). A coherent
$\ell{=}2$ gravitational-wave burst is emitted---propagating at $v\!=\!c$, hence
genuine spin-2 radiation rather than the sub-luminal massive scalar---sourced purely
by the rotation and the collapse. Because the source is a NEC-violating phantom field,
the trapped region subsequently rebounds (a ``phantom bounce''), leaving a dynamical
remnant rather than a quiescent black hole. The Hamiltonian and momentum constraints remain bounded
($L_2\!\lesssim\!3.3\times10^{-2}$ and $9.7\times10^{-3}$) through $t=40\,M$ with no
numerical failure. We emphasize that this is an \emph{engineered} spacetime, not a
solution of the pure Einstein--Klein--Gordon system, and we discuss the trust level,
caveats, and astrophysical implications of collapsing rotating exotic compact
objects.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Traversable wormholes require matter that violates the null energy condition
(NEC)~\cite{morris88}, and the canonical static solution is the Ellis--Bronnikov
phantom-scalar bridge~\cite{ellis73,bronnikov73}. Such geometries are dynamically
unstable~\cite{shinkai02,gonzalez09}: compressive perturbations force collapse,
rarefactive ones force runaway expansion. A companion study~\cite{shirokov_wh_dynamics}
evolved the \emph{static} Ellis--Bronnikov wormhole in 3D and showed that reducing
the phantom support ($S_{\rm support}=0.5$) together with a quadrupolar scalar kick
drives a clean collapse with $v\!\approx\!c$ gravitational radiation.

Here we take the next step and ask a sharper question: \emph{can a rotating exotic
throat, held open by an engineered support source, be made to collapse on command,
and does it radiate gravitational waves without any artificially imposed
quadrupole?} A rotating configuration is qualitatively different from the static
case for two reasons. First, rotation supplies its own time-dependent, non-axisymmetric
mass-energy distribution, so an $\ell{=}2$ signal can be sourced by the collapse
itself---no kick is required. Second, a rotating exotic lump twisted into an $m{=}1$
torus is \emph{not} a stationary eigenstate of the coupled system; the throat-sphere
Noether charge drains with a half-life of $t\!\approx\!13$--$16\,M$ and the spacetime
relaxes to flat rather than collapsing. To obtain a controlled experiment we
therefore replace the missing stationarity with active control: a PD ``matter
pump'' holds the throat open, and switching it off triggers the collapse.

This is, by construction, an \emph{engineered} (actively supported) wormhole rather
than a self-consistent solution of the Einstein--Klein--Gordon system. The pump is a
deliberate violation of the contracted Bianchi identity, so constraint drift must be
monitored throughout. Within these clearly stated limits, the experiment isolates a
clean physical result: the removal of support from a rotating exotic throat produces
gravitational collapse to a trapped surface and a coherent $\ell{=}2$ burst.

\section{Formalism and Methodology}

\subsection{3+1 decomposition and the CCZ4 evolution}
We solve the Einstein equations $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (with $G=c=1$) as a
Cauchy problem in the 3+1 decomposition~\cite{baumgarte10}. Spacetime is foliated
into spacelike slices $\Sigma_t$ with line element
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = -\alpha^2\,dt^2 + \gamma_{ij}\,(dx^i+\beta^i dt)(dx^j+\beta^j dt),
\label{eq:adm}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is the lapse, $\beta^i$ the shift, and $\gamma_{ij}$ the spatial
metric. The coupled Einstein--(complex-)scalar system is evolved with
\texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}, a GPU-accelerated, AMReX-based port of
\texttt{GRChombo}~\cite{grchombo}, using the conformal and covariant Z4 (CCZ4)
formulation with matter sources. As in the moving-puncture approach, the spatial
metric is conformally decomposed,
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{ij}=\chi^{-1}\tilde\gamma_{ij},\qquad \det\tilde\gamma_{ij}=1,
\qquad \chi=(\det\gamma_{ij})^{-1/3},
\end{equation}
and the extrinsic curvature is split into its trace $K$ and conformal
trace-free part $\tilde A_{ij}$. The regularized variable $\chi$ (rather than a
conformal factor $\psi$ that diverges at a throat/puncture) is what makes the
compactified interior numerically tractable: $\chi\to0$ marks infinite metric
stretching at the throat, while a collapsing lapse $\alpha\to0$ marks black-hole
formation. The CCZ4 constraint-damping terms propagate and dissipate any
Hamiltonian/momentum residual; we use $1+\log$ slicing for $\alpha$ and a
Gamma-driver for $\beta^i$, both from moving-puncture initial gauge
($\alpha(0)=1$, $\beta^i(0)=0$).

\subsection{Initial data and metric setup: not the Teo geometry}
\label{sec:metric}
It is important to state precisely what geometry we evolve, because it is
\emph{not} the analytic Teo stationary rotating vacuum wormhole~\cite{teo98}. We
instead build a rotating exotic-matter configuration on an
Ellis--Bronnikov/Kleihaus--Kunz-class throat~\cite{kleihaus14} and then solve the
full constraint system numerically. Concretely:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Geometry.} The seed spatial slice is a conformally-flat, singular-$\psi$
(puncture-like) throat of the Ellis--Bronnikov type. In isotropic coordinates
$(\bar r,\theta,\varphi)$ the spatial line element is
\begin{equation}
d\ell^2 = \psi^4\big[d\bar r^2 + \bar r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)\big],
\qquad
\psi = \sqrt{\,1+\frac{b_0^2}{4\bar r^2}\,},
\label{eq:throat_metric}
\end{equation}
with throat radius $b_0$, so that the evolved conformal variable is
\begin{equation}
\chi = \psi^{-4} = \left(\frac{4\bar r^2}{4\bar r^2 + b_0^2}\right)^{2}\;\xrightarrow{\ \bar r\to0\ }\;0 ,
\label{eq:chi_throat}
\end{equation}
a genuine topological bridge $\mathbb{R}\times S^2$ whose second asymptotic region is
compactified to $\bar r\to0$ and regularized through $\chi\to0$, exactly as in the
static companion study~\cite{shirokov_wh_dynamics}, rather than an analytic rotating
vacuum metric. The full spacetime line element is the 3+1 form of Eq.~(\ref{eq:adm}); at $t=0$
the shift and extrinsic curvature ($\beta^i$, $K_{ij}$) are not prescribed but are
\emph{solved} from the momentum constraint sourced by the rotating matter, and the
Hamiltonian solve corrects $\psi$ away from the vacuum seed Eq.~(\ref{eq:throat_metric}).
\item \textbf{Rotation lives in the matter, not an imposed metric $g_{t\varphi}$.}
Instead of prescribing an off-diagonal metric rotation, the angular momentum is
carried by the matter: a rotating complex Q-torus (Sec.~\ref{sec:matter}) supplies
the $J_z$ and the non-axisymmetric structure. The metric rotation ($\beta^i$,
$g_{t\varphi}$) is then \emph{generated self-consistently} by solving the momentum
constraint for this matter, rather than assumed.
\item \textbf{Constraint-clean by construction.} This is the decisive methodological
point: analytic rotating data with $K_{ij}=0$ carry an $\mathcal{O}(\omega)$
momentum-constraint defect that grows and destroys the evolution
(Sec.~\ref{sec:why_matter}). We therefore \emph{solve} both the Hamiltonian and
momentum constraints numerically for the exact throat+matter configuration.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Why this choice over the Teo metric.} The decision is driven by what a
\emph{dynamical} (Cauchy) evolution actually requires, and the analytic Teo geometry
fails these requirements on four counts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An evolvable matter source, not a reverse-engineered one.} The Teo line
element is a stationary metric \emph{ansatz}; the stress-energy needed to support it
is obtained by reading off $G_{\mu\nu}/8\pi$, and it is generically an anisotropic,
exotic fluid with no accompanying field Lagrangian. To evolve the coupled system one
needs matter with its own equations of motion. Our complex scalar $\Phi$ supplies
exactly that: a Klein--Gordon field that is evolved self-consistently alongside the
metric, so the source is closed and physical rather than inferred after the fact.
\item \textbf{Constraint satisfaction on a Cartesian grid.} Numerical relativity
requires initial data $(\gamma_{ij},K_{ij})$ that satisfy the Hamiltonian and
momentum constraints. Interpolating an analytic rotating metric onto the grid (with
a truncated or vanishing $K_{ij}$) leaves an $\mathcal{O}(\omega)$ constraint
residual that we found grows and crashes the run at $t\approx2.8\,M$; the same code
is stable when the data are constraint-solved (Sec.~\ref{sec:why_matter}). The
elliptic solve is not a convenience---it is what makes a rotating evolution possible
at all.
\item \textbf{Reuse of a proven, regular puncture representation.} The singular-$\psi$
throat regularized through $\chi=\psi^{-4}\to0$ is the representation already
validated in the static companion study~\cite{shirokov_wh_dynamics}, and it maps the
infinite second asymptotic region onto a single grid point cleanly. Teo's native
coordinates (with off-diagonal $g_{t\varphi}$ and proper-radius structure) do not map
onto this moving-puncture-regularized Cartesian grid without additional, error-prone
transformations.
\item \textbf{A physical carrier of angular momentum, and a control knob.} Placing
rotation in a matter current ($S_i=-\Pi\,\partial_i\phi$, $U(1)$ Noether charge)
gives the momentum constraint a genuine source, so the shift is \emph{solved} rather
than imposed, and it provides the support/collapse ``knob'' central to the
engineered, collapse-on-command program (Sec.~\ref{sec:matter}). A fixed vacuum
metric offers no matter degree of freedom to ramp, and hence no controllable trigger.
\end{enumerate}
In short, the Teo geometry answers ``what stationary rotating wormhole metric exists''
whereas our question is ``what happens dynamically when support is withdrawn from a
rotating exotic throat''---which demands evolvable matter and constraint-clean data,
neither of which the analytic metric provides.

The initial data and the evolution are produced by two distinct codes chained
together (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}), so that what \texttt{GRTeclyn} evolves is
genuinely constraint-satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Solve (\texttt{GRTresna}).} The exotic complex-winding Q-torus is
painted onto the singular-$\psi$ throat, and the \texttt{GRTresna} elliptic solver
(a \texttt{GRChombo}-family, MPI initial-data code) solves the Lichnerowicz
Hamiltonian constraint for the conformal factor together with the vector-potential
form of the momentum constraint for the shift/$\tilde A_{ij}$ sourced by the
rotating matter. For this run the solve converges to a Hamiltonian residual
$\lesssim1\%$.
\item \textbf{Flatten (\texttt{convert\_chombo\_to\_gridinit}).} The solved,
possibly AMR, \texttt{GRTresna} output is resampled onto a uniform grid at the
evolution's level-0 resolution and written as a portable \texttt{.gridinit} file.
A hard lesson enforced here (``L2'') is to \emph{never} evolve on a finer mesh than
the initial-data solve, which would re-introduce an interpolation constraint
defect; the run's $dx$ therefore fixes both the solve resolution and the evolution
level-0 spacing.
\item \textbf{Evolve (\texttt{GRTeclyn}).} \texttt{GRTeclyn} loads the
\texttt{.gridinit}, reconstructs the CCZ4 state and the two-channel complex scalar
$(\phi_1,\Pi_1,\phi_2,\Pi_2)$, and evolves the coupled system on a GPU with adaptive
mesh refinement, applying the support pump and its ramp
(Sec.~\ref{sec:matter}), the sponge, and the in-flight diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}
The essential virtue of this arrangement is that \emph{everything
\texttt{GRTeclyn} evolves is clean and constraint-solved}. The elliptic solve
enforces the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints on the exact throat$+$matter
configuration \emph{before} any time stepping, the flatten step preserves that
solution at the evolution resolution (never refining below the initial-data
spacing), and only then does the hyperbolic evolution begin. There is therefore no
$t{=}0$ constraint defect to seed spurious growth---the residual is at solver
tolerance ($\lesssim1\%$ Hamiltonian) rather than the $\mathcal{O}(\omega)$ violation
that an interpolated analytic rotating metric would carry---and the bounded
constraint norms reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:trust} are a direct consequence of this
clean, constraint-solved start.

An alternative, purely geometric collapse trigger is also available: solving the
constraints for a scaled matter amplitude $f\!\to\!\kappa f$ yields, for $\kappa<1$,
an \emph{exact} Einstein solution out of equilibrium (zero initial constraint
defect) that collapses on its own. The run analyzed here uses the equilibrium
$\kappa=1$ data and triggers collapse dynamically through the support ramp instead.

\subsection{Matter: a rotating exotic Q-ball torus}
\label{sec:matter}
The throat is threaded by a \emph{complex} scalar field $\Phi$ with a self-interacting
potential
\begin{equation}
V(|\Phi|^2)=\tfrac{1}{2}\mu^2|\Phi|^2-\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda|\Phi|^4
+\tfrac{1}{6}\mu_6|\Phi|^6,
\end{equation}
with $\mu^2$ (encoded as $\mathtt{phantom\_mass}=0.5$), $\lambda=170$, and
$\mu_6=1.445\times10^4$. The sextic term stabilizes a finite-amplitude soliton
(Q-ball). The field carries an azimuthal winding $\Phi=f(\rho,z)\,e^{i(m\varphi-\omega
t)}$ with $m=1$ and rotation frequency $\omega=0.2507$. The modulus $f(\rho,z)$ is a
\emph{genuine} stationary spinning-Q-ball eigenstate: it solves the 2D axisymmetric
Klein--Gordon eigenvalue problem $\nabla^2 f-(m^2/\rho^2+\kappa^2)f+\lambda
f^3-\mu_6 f^5=0$ (with $\kappa^2=\mu^2-\omega^2$), obtained by a damped-Newton solve
under $\omega$-continuation from the near-linear thick-wall regime. This is what
distinguishes the final construction from an earlier, dispersive attempt that merely
twisted a \emph{spherical} lump into a torus (Sec.~\ref{sec:why_matter}). The result
is a rotating Q-ball \emph{torus} carrying a conserved $U(1)$ Noether charge. Crucially, the
gravitational coupling is exotic: the stress-energy enters with the phantom sign so
that the configuration violates the NEC and can hold a throat open, exactly as in
the Ellis--Bronnikov case, but now with rotation and a confining potential. The
initial data---geometry \emph{and} the exotic matter profile---are produced by the
\texttt{GRTresna} elliptic solver so that the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints
are satisfied to solver tolerance at $t=0$.

\subsection{Why this matter model: a decision chain forced by the physics}
\label{sec:why_matter}
The choice of a \emph{constraint-solved, self-interacting, complex, exotic Q-torus}
was not aesthetic; each ingredient was forced by a concrete failure of the simpler
alternatives, documented in our development log. We summarize the chain because it
explains what the matter must be and why nothing cheaper works.

\emph{(i) Exotic (phantom) coupling---to hold a throat open.} As in the static
Ellis--Bronnikov case, keeping a throat open requires NEC violation, so the scalar
enters the field equations with the phantom stress-energy sign.

\emph{(ii) Complex, not real---to rotate coherently.} A genuine rotation requires an
azimuthal phase, $\Phi\propto e^{i(m\varphi-\omega t)}$, which a single real scalar
cannot carry. Our first attempt (a real-scalar Gaussian lump) produced a spurious
four-lobe artifact rather than a smooth rotating torus. A \emph{complex} field with
two channels $(\phi_1,\Pi_1,\phi_2,\Pi_2)$ carries a smooth $U(1)$ current and a
conserved Noether charge---the natural way to encode rotation.

\emph{(iii) Constraint-solved, not analytic---to avoid a numerical instability
masquerading as physics.} Analytic rotating data with $K_{ij}=0$ carry an
$\mathcal{O}(\omega)$ momentum-constraint defect. In direct tests the analytic
rotating configuration began with a $\sim\!3\times$ larger Hamiltonian defect that
\emph{grew} and produced a \texttt{NaN} at $t\approx2.8\,M$, while the \emph{same}
code in the static limit was stable (constraints decreased, throat held). Replacing
the analytic data with a \texttt{GRTresna} elliptic solve removed the defect: the
rotating equilibrium then held with \emph{decreasing} constraints and a
\emph{conserved} $J_z$. The lesson---the rotating ``instability'' of the naive
setup was numerical, not physical---is why constraint-clean initial data are
mandatory here.

\emph{(iv) A bound eigenstate (Q-ball), not a Gaussian or a bare-mass lump---to stop
the matter dispersing.} A Gaussian profile is a superposition of many radial modes;
the non-eigenstate part simply radiates away, with or without a mass term (a bare
mass only shifts the dispersion relation). Confinement requires the \emph{profile}
itself to be a genuine bound state. Of the two candidates, a self-gravitating boson
star is bound by gravity---but under the phantom sign self-gravity is effectively
repulsive, so it is \emph{not} bound. Only a \emph{Q-ball}, bound by its own
self-interaction potential $V(|\Phi|^2)$, binds through a mechanism independent of
the gravitational sign. This forces the sextic, U(1)-symmetric potential above; a
per-component (separable) potential would break the $U(1)$ symmetry and disperse the
field immediately, so the coupled form on $|\Phi|^2$ is required. The correct
confinement diagnostic is the Noether charge inside a fixed coordinate sphere
($Q_{\rm sphere}$), not the whole-box density sum $\rho_{\rm sum}$, which is
misleading on a unigrid.

\emph{(v) Why active support is still needed.} An early attempt that merely twisted a
\emph{spherical} Q-ball into an $m{=}1$ torus was not a true \emph{rotating}
eigenstate: the throat charge $Q_{\rm sphere}$ drained with a half-life
$t\approx13$--$16\,M$ and the throat relaxed \emph{toward flat space} rather than
collapsing. Solving the genuine 2D spinning-Q-ball eigenstate (Sec.~\ref{sec:matter})
removes this dispersive channel in flat space, but two obstacles remain on the curved,
gravitating throat. First, a flat-space eigenstate is detuned by the throat geometry
and self-gravity, so it is not exactly stationary once embedded. Second, there is an
amplitude/existence clash: the throat
constraint solve converges only at small exotic amplitude ($\mathrm{amp}\approx0.1$),
whereas a Q-ball is an eigenstate only near its natural central amplitude
$\phi_c\sim\mathcal{O}(1)$ (forcing the amplitude higher pushes the solve outside the
Lichnerowicz existence boundary, $\mathrm{Ham}\!\sim\!85\%$). Slow rotation and
throat convergence therefore pull the amplitude in opposite directions, and no
single passive lump closes the gap. This is precisely why we supply the missing
stationarity \emph{actively}, with the support pump described next---and why the
resulting object is an engineered, controllable throat rather than a passive
eigenstate.

\subsection{Active support and the collapse trigger}
For the reasons in Sec.~\ref{sec:why_matter}(v), even the genuine Q-torus is not
exactly stationary once embedded on the gravitating throat. We therefore
add a proportional--derivative ``matter pump'': a spatially localized,
closed-loop source term that drives the field toward a target soliton profile,
holding the throat open (support strength $S=1$). This is the engineered element of
the configuration. Collapse is triggered by a smooth ramp of the support to zero,
\begin{equation}
S(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[1+\cos\!\Big(\pi\,\tfrac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}\Big)\right],
\quad t\in[t_0,t_1]=[8,10]\,M,
\end{equation}
with $S=0$ for $t\ge t_1$. Removing the exotic support lets gravity win, the
rotating analogue of the static $S_{\rm support}\!\to\!0$ branch. \textbf{No}
quadrupolar or monopolar scalar perturbation is imposed
($\mathtt{wormhole\_phi\_perturbation\_amplitude}=0$): any $\ell{=}2$ radiation is
sourced by the rotation and the collapse itself.

\subsection{Grid, boundaries, and horizon proxy}
The domain is a cube of side $L=64$ with $N=128$ base cells ($dx=0.5$) and up to
$\mathtt{max\_level}=3$ levels of adaptive refinement centered on the throat at
$(32,32,32)$. An outer sponge zone (radially ramped Kreiss--Oliger dissipation,
$r\in[28,30]$) absorbs outgoing waves before the Sommerfeld boundary. As in the
companion work, in the absence of a production horizon finder we use a
trapped-surface proxy on coordinate spheres,
\begin{equation}
\theta_+ = \frac{2\sqrt{\chi}}{\bar r}-\frac{\partial_{\bar r}\chi}{\sqrt{\chi}}
+\tilde A_{rr}-\tfrac{2}{3}K,
\end{equation}
identifying a trapped surface when $\theta_+\le0$; the largest such radius defines
the apparent-horizon proxy $r_{\rm AH}$.

\subsection{Gravitational-wave extraction}
The Weyl scalar $\Psi_4$ is projected onto spin-weighted spherical harmonics
${}_{-2}Y_{\ell m}$ on spheres at $R_{\rm ext}\in\{12,18,24\}$. We report the
dominant $\ell{=}2,m{=}0$ mode. \textbf{Important:} we use the \emph{post-hoc Python
extraction} that samples the $\Psi_4$ derived field from output plotfiles, because
\texttt{GRTeclyn}'s \emph{in-code} C++ Weyl/CCE extraction is a feature the GRTL
collaboration currently lists as ``in progress'' and which we found to be unreliable
for this run (it carries a spurious $\mathcal{O}(1)$ $m{=}0$ imaginary part, whereas
a correct $m{=}0$ projection must be essentially real). The Python extraction passes
this null test: $\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_4^{2,0}$ has RMS $\sim\!10^{-5}$, confirming a
clean, physically valid projection.

\subsection{Collapse and horizon formation}
Figure~\ref{fig:evolution} shows the qualitative evolution and
Fig.~\ref{fig:diagnostics} the quantitative fine-level diagnostics. While the pump is
on ($t<8\,M$) the throat is held in quasi-equilibrium. Once the support ramps off,
gravity wins and the throat collapses:
\begin{itemize}
\item the minimum lapse falls monotonically from $\min(\alpha)=1.000$ to
$0.033$, the signature of a collapsing region;
\item the minimum conformal factor pinches to $\min(\chi)=0.019$, i.e.\ extreme
metric stretching at the throat;
\item the maximum extrinsic curvature grows to $\max|K|\approx0.62$;
\item a trapped surface forms: the apparent-horizon proxy reaches
$r_{\rm AH}\approx9.2$ with $\min\theta_+\approx-1.9\le0$.
\end{itemize}
The lapse collapse, the $\chi$ pinch, and the trapped surface together constitute the
formation of a nascent black hole (an apparent horizon), enacting cosmic censorship
around the crushed exotic core.

\emph{Final state: matter capture and a phantom bounce.} The exotic matter is
\emph{captured}, not radiated away. The conserved $U(1)$ Noether charge inside the
$r<10$ sphere stays $Q_{\rm sphere}\approx-1.6$ to $-1.9$ throughout (dipping only to
$\approx-1.2$ near $t\approx25$--$30\,M$), so the bulk of the matter is crushed inward
with the throat and held within the trapped region rather than escaping. The trapped
surface itself is present for $\approx94\%$ of the run (first at $t\approx2.3\,M$,
robustly from $t\approx12\,M$), and the lapse deepens to $\min(\alpha)\approx10^{-3}$
by $t\approx30\,M$. However---because the source is a NEC-violating phantom field
with negative pressure---the collapse is \emph{contested} rather than quiescent: the
apparent-horizon proxy grows to $r_{\rm AH}\approx9.2$ at $t\approx15\,M$ and then
\emph{shrinks back} monotonically ($8.5\to6.7\to4.8\to3.2\to1.9$ at
$t=20,25,30,35,40\,M$), while the lapse partially recovers ($\min\alpha\!:\,10^{-3}
\!\to\!0.033$) and $\chi$ un-pinches slightly ($0.019\to0.049$). This is the rotating
analogue of the ``phantom bounce'' seen in the static collapse~\cite{shirokov_wh_dynamics}:
the crushed exotic matter's repulsive pressure pushes back against the compression, so
the remnant is a dynamical, bouncing object rather than a settling Schwarzschild black
hole. Two caveats temper this final-state picture: $r_{\rm AH}$ is a coordinate-sphere
\emph{proxy} rather than the output of a true apparent-horizon finder, and the late
phase---where gradients are steepest---is the least resolution-converged part of a
single-resolution run. The robust statement is that a trapped surface and a collapsing
lapse form and capture the exotic matter; the detailed late-time fate awaits a
convergence study and a genuine horizon finder.

\subsection{Gravitational-wave emission}
The extracted $\Psi_4^{2,0}$ waveform is smooth and coherent
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gw}). A near-zone/gauge response appears at $t\approx6$--$8\,M$
(around the support ramp), followed by the physical burst that develops as the throat
crushes ($t\approx13$--$24\,M$) and then decays. The signal is causally consistent
with the trigger: support is removed at $t\in[8,10]\,M$, the throat collapses, and the
resulting quadrupole radiation reaches the inner detector at $R_{\rm ext}=12$ at the
expected retarded time. The dominant radiated power is at low frequency
($f\lesssim0.1\,M^{-1}$), so the coarse dense sampling of the post-hoc extraction is
more than sufficient to resolve it.

Scaling to physical units, the peak strain frequency of the burst is $\approx169$~Hz
for a stellar-mass remnant ($M=30\,M_\odot$)---squarely in the Advanced LIGO band---and
$\approx5$~Hz for an intermediate-mass system ($M=10^3\,M_\odot$), in the
decihertz/DECIGO band. As in the static case, the morphology is a short, localized
burst rather than a long inspiral chirp, favoring unmodeled burst pipelines or
numerically generated templates.

\subsection{Is the signal gravitational radiation, or dispersing matter?}
\label{sec:gw_vs_matter}
Because the trigger removes the support that was confining an exotic lump, one must
ask whether the extracted signal is genuine gravitational radiation or simply the
unsupported scalar matter streaming outward past the detectors. The diagnostics give
a clear, if nuanced, answer.

\emph{What the matter does.} The conserved $U(1)$ Noether charge inside a fixed
$r<10$ sphere, $Q_{\rm sphere}$, is \emph{largely retained} through the collapse
(it stays $\approx-1.6$ to $-1.9$, dipping only to $\approx-1.2$), i.e.\ the bulk of
the ``particle number'' is crushed inward with the throat rather than expelled. At
the same time, the collapse and the ensuing phantom-pressure rebound drive an
\emph{outward-propagating scalar/curvature shock}: the scalar-activity RMS radius
grows from $8.3$ to $22$ and the fraction of activity confined within $r<10$ falls
from $0.81$ to $0.04$. So a genuine outward matter disturbance does exist---but it is
a shock of field gradients, not a net escape of charge.

\emph{Why the extracted $\Psi_4$ is nonetheless gravitational.} Three independent
points separate the gravitational-wave signal from the matter flux:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\Psi_4$ is a curvature scalar.} It is a Weyl-tensor projection---the
\emph{free} (spin-2) gravitational field. Even at points threaded by scalar matter it
carries only the Weyl part; the local Ricci/matter piece is projected out. It is
therefore not the scalar field $\phi$ in disguise.
\item \textbf{Speed test.} The extracted $\Psi_4^{2,0}$ burst propagates at
\emph{exactly} $v=1.00\,c$ between $R_{\rm ext}=12$, $18$, and $24$ (retarded-time
lags of $6$ and $12\,M$ over $\Delta r=6$ and $12$). The scalar field here is
\emph{massive} ($\mu^2=0.5$) and self-interacting, so scalar wavepackets travel
\emph{sub-luminally} and disperse; they cannot produce a coherent $v=c$ front. The
$v=c$ propagation is the signature of massless gravitational radiation.
\item \textbf{Causal, not identical.} The two are causally linked---the
non-axisymmetric (rotating $m{=}1$) collapsing and rebounding matter carries a
time-varying quadrupole that \emph{sources} the $\ell{=}2$ radiation---but the
radiated field ($\Psi_4$, $v=c$) is physically distinct from the matter shock
(massive, $v<c$).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{The real systematic.} At \emph{finite} extraction radii the raw $\Psi_4$ also
contains a \emph{near-zone} gravitational response to the passing matter (a
non-radiative, locally sourced term)---this is the origin of the small $r{=}12$ bump
near $t\approx9\,M$, coincident with the support ramp. The $v=c$ propagation test and
the multi-radius retarded-time alignment are precisely what isolate the genuinely
radiative part from this near-zone contamination. A fully clean separation would use
wave-zone extraction at larger radii or Cauchy--characteristic extraction, which we
list as future work. In summary: the matter is mostly crushed inward (with an outward
dispersing shock), and the recorded gravitational wave is a true spin-2 signal
\emph{sourced by} that matter's quadrupole---not the diffusing matter itself.

\subsection{Can we trust the results?}
\label{sec:trust}
The evolution is numerically healthy in the sense that matters most for this claim:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Constraints stay bounded.} The volume-weighted $L_2$ Hamiltonian norm
never exceeds $3.3\times10^{-2}$ and the momentum norm $9.7\times10^{-3}$; both are
flat through $t=40\,M$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:constraints}). The run completes without
\texttt{NaN} or grid failure.
\item \textbf{The GW extraction passes a null test.} The $m{=}0$ imaginary part is
$\sim\!10^{-5}$ (must be $\approx0$), confirming the projection is physically sound.
\item \textbf{The collapse is unambiguous.} A trapped surface and a collapsing lapse
are robust, gauge-insensitive indicators of horizon formation.
\end{itemize}
Three honest caveats bound the interpretation. (i) This is a \emph{single-resolution}
study ($dx=0.5$, $\mathtt{max\_level}=3$); it is not a convergence-tested measurement,
so radiated energies and any quasinormal frequencies are indicative, not precision
values. (ii) The engineered pump violates the Bianchi identity by design; while the
constraints remain bounded, angular momentum and Noether charge are \emph{not}
conserved during the run ($J_z$ and $Q$ drift by $\gtrsim100\%$ and change sign),
plausibly a combination of radiated and pump-injected contributions that we have not
separated. (iii) The in-code C++ $\Psi_4$ extraction is unreliable (an incomplete
upstream feature), which is why all physics here rests on the post-hoc Python
extraction.

\subsection{Is this an engineered configuration?}
Yes, and explicitly so. Unlike the exact Ellis--Bronnikov bridge, a rotating $m{=}1$
Q-torus is not a stationary eigenstate; left alone it disperses. The PD matter pump
is an active control source that supplies the missing stationarity to hold the throat
open, and the collapse is produced by switching that source off. The result should
therefore be read as a controlled \emph{dynamical experiment}---``what happens when
support is withdrawn from a rotating exotic throat''---rather than as evidence that
such a rotating wormhole exists as a self-consistent vacuum-plus-matter solution. The
value of the experiment is that it cleanly isolates the collapse dynamics and the
attendant, kick-free $\ell{=}2$ radiation.

\section{Discussion and Implications}
The central result is qualitative and, we believe, robust to the caveats above:
\emph{withdrawing support from a rotating exotic throat drives gravitational collapse
to a trapped surface and emits a coherent $\ell{=}2$ gravitational-wave burst, with no
imposed quadrupole kick.} This is the rotating, engineered analogue of the static
Ellis--Bronnikov collapse branch, and it demonstrates that the rotation itself
supplies the asymmetry needed to radiate.

Astrophysically, this maps onto a natural scenario. If primordial exotic
configurations were held open during an NEC-violating (e.g.\ phantom-inflationary)
epoch and lost that support at reheating---modeled here by the support ramp---rotating
examples would collapse and radiate. Because the source is rotating, rapidly spinning
cases could additionally develop bar-mode ($m{=}2$) instabilities, a distinct channel
we did not probe here but which the machinery supports. The burst is short and
localized in time, so searches would rely on unmodeled burst pipelines or on
numerically generated collapse templates injected into matched-filter analyses; the
mass scaling places stellar-mass remnants in the Advanced LIGO band and
supermassive ones in the LISA band.

\noindent\textbf{Future work.} The priorities are (i) a resolution ladder to convert
the indicative frequencies and energies into convergence-tested numbers; (ii) an
angular-momentum/charge budget that separates radiated from pump-injected $J_z$ and
$Q$; (iii) completing or replacing the in-code C++ Weyl/CCE extraction so dense,
consistent waveforms can be produced during the run rather than post-hoc; and (iv)
probing the rapidly rotating regime for $m{=}2$ bar-mode emission.
