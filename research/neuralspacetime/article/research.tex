\title{\MakeTextUppercase{Matter-First Discovery and Numerical Validation of a
Transient Null-Geodesic Shortcut}}

\author{\MakeTextUppercase{Nikita M. Shirokov}}
\email{shirokov.nm@phystech.edu}
\affiliation{\textit{Independent Researcher, Moscow, Russia}}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We use a matter-first numerical-relativity pipeline to search for transient
spacetimes that shorten null travel times relative to flat space between fixed
endpoints. MAP-Elites proposes five-lump complex-scalar configurations,
\texttt{GRTresna} solves the Einstein constraints, and \texttt{GRTeclyn} evolves
the resulting data. The principal candidate, \texttt{eval\,118}, is a breathing,
retrograde shell containing predominantly exotic scalar matter. In a production
replay with domain size $L=128$, base resolution $N=256$, three refinement
levels, and final time $t=30$, an evolving four-dimensional null-geodesic sweep
finds a peak shortcut
$f_{\rm geo}=17.38\%$ at emission time $t_{\rm emit}=8$, with all five rays
reaching the receiver and maximum relative null-Hamiltonian drift
$1.69\times10^{-3}$. The signal remains positive in five completed validation
runs: $17.27$--$19.63\%$ across $N=192,256,384$ and $L=96,128,160$.
The fine--medium difference is $5.4\%$, while changing $L=128$ to $160$
changes the result by $0.36\%$. The resolution sequence is non-monotonic, so
Richardson extrapolation and a continuum value are not claimed. Constraint
norms remain bounded, but the matter disperses to about $25\%$ confinement by
$t=30$. The result is therefore a numerically robust transient null shortcut
supported by exotic matter, not a stable or practical warp drive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Warp geometries are commonly studied by prescribing a metric and inferring the
stress tensor required to support it~\cite{alcubierre94,bobrick21}. That approach
does not by itself show that compatible initial data exist or that the geometry
survives dynamical evolution. We instead search over matter configurations,
solve the constraints, evolve the complete Einstein--matter system, and test
transport with null rays in the time-dependent metric.

Here ``shortcut'' means earlier arrival than a flat-space ray connecting the
same asymptotic endpoints. No material object or light ray moves outside its
local null cone. The central result is not a large coordinate light speed but a
repeatable travel-time advantage measured by an evolving four-dimensional
geodesic tracer.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Matter-first search}

The search campaign \texttt{qball\_traj\_spiral\_v2} used MAP-Elites
quality-diversity optimization~\cite{mouret15} over a 39-parameter family of five
complex-scalar lumps. Each candidate specifies radii, phases, tilts, radial and
azimuthal motion, exotic signs, breathing, and vertical oscillation.
\texttt{GRTresna} constructs constraint-solved initial data; candidates failing
the post-load Hamiltonian or momentum gate are rejected before evolution.
Of 200 attempted candidates, 133 completed GPU evolution and nine occupied the
$8\times8$ behavior archive. Candidate \texttt{eval\,118} had the largest
search score, $603.39$.

The scalar seed uses mass $m=1$, quartic coupling $\lambda=640$, parameter
$\mu=85333$, frequency $\omega=0.8$, and an $m=1$ ODE profile. Four lumps have
exotic fractions $0.75$--$0.91$ and one has $0.32$. All rotate retrograde, with
$\omega_{\rm rot}=-0.04$ to $-0.15$, while a collective breathing mode and
vertical oscillation drive the shell. These parameters define an engineered
initial-data family rather than a stationary boson-star solution.

\subsection{Evolution and validation matrix}

We evolve the CCZ4 system with \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn,grchombo}.
The headline run (RM) uses $L=128$, $N=256$, base spacing $h=0.5$, three
refinement levels, and $t_{\rm stop}=30$. Resolution runs use the same domain
with $N=192$ (RC), $256$ (RM), and $384$ (RF). Domain runs use matched
$h=0.5$ with $(L,N)=(96,192)$ (DS), $(128,256)$ (RM), and $(160,320)$ (DL).
All five simulations completed with exit code zero.

\subsection{Evolving null-geodesic diagnostic}

Null rays obey
\begin{equation}
 \dot x^\mu=g^{\mu\nu}k_\nu,\qquad
 \dot k_\mu=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu g^{\alpha\beta}k_\alpha k_\beta .
\end{equation}
They are integrated through an interpolated stack of evolved metric slices.
For each emission time we define
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm geo}=\max\left(0,
 \frac{t_{\rm flat}-\Delta t_{\rm curved}}{t_{\rm flat}}\right).
\end{equation}
Seven launches at $t_{\rm emit}=0,2,\ldots,12$ use five rays each. A result is
accepted only when all rays reach the receiver and the relative null-Hamiltonian
drift remains below $10^{-2}$. Frozen-slice geodesics are retained as
diagnostics but are not the transport certificate.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Null-shortcut certificate}

The RM sweep gives $f_{\rm geo}=12.12,15.13,16.65,13.81,17.38,13.84,$ and
$14.69\%$ for successive emission times. The maximum occurs at
$t_{\rm emit}=8$; all $5/5$ rays arrive and the maximum relative Hamiltonian
drift is $1.69\times10^{-3}$. The corresponding effective endpoint speed is
$c/(1-f_{\rm geo})\simeq1.21c$, although every ray remains locally null.

The validation values are
\[
\begin{array}{lcccc}
\text{run} & L & N & h & f_{\rm geo}^{\rm peak}\\
\hline
\mathrm{RC} &128&192&0.667&19.63\%\\
\mathrm{RM} &128&256&0.500&17.38\%\\
\mathrm{RF} &128&384&0.333&18.38\%\\
\mathrm{DS} & 96&192&0.500&17.27\%\\
\mathrm{DL} &160&320&0.500&17.45\%
\end{array}
\]
RF differs from RM by $5.4\%$ relative, below the predeclared $10\%$
acceptance tolerance. DL differs from RM by $0.36\%$, and DS by $0.63\%$.
The domain dependence is therefore small over $L=96$--$160$.

\subsection{Resolution behavior and constraints}

For a controlled Richardson estimate one would require
\begin{equation}
 Q(h)=Q_0+Ch^p
\end{equation}
with a consistent sign of successive differences. The resolution sequence
$19.63\%\rightarrow17.38\%\rightarrow18.38\%$ is non-monotonic; consequently
no positive order $p$, continuum value, or grid-convergence index is reported.
The evidence supports robustness within a finite-resolution band, not
asymptotic convergence.

In RM, the volume-weighted norms remain below
\[
 \|\mathcal H\|_2=5.31\times10^{-3},\qquad
 \|\mathcal M\|_2=7.48\times10^{-4}
\]
through $t=30$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:constraints}). Across RC, RM, and RF the maxima
are $5.335,5.313,5.317\times10^{-3}$ and
$7.497,7.478,7.478\times10^{-4}$, respectively. These are bounded and nearly
resolution-independent, but also non-monotonic or plateaued; they do not
justify Richardson extrapolation.

\subsection{Matter persistence and preliminary curvature signal}

The numerical evolution survives, but the matter structure does not remain
compact. At $t=30$ the RM confinement fraction is $25.6\%$ and its rms spread
has grown by a factor $2.76$. The corresponding values across the five-run
matrix are $23$--$26\%$. The shortcut is therefore transient and cannot be
interpreted as a durable transport corridor.

Post-processed spherical extraction finds an $\ell=2,m=0$ curvature signal at
$R_{\rm ext}=12,18,24$, with peak
$|r\Psi_4^{2,0}|\simeq2.05\times10^{-2}$ at the innermost radius
(Fig.~\ref{fig:psi4}). The RM record contains only 22 samples. It is sufficient
to confirm that extraction is active, but not to support a spectral,
quasinormal-mode, energy, or detector claim. A dedicated $L=160$, $t=80$ run
with denser output and radii $24,36,48$ is reserved for that analysis.

\section{Discussion}

The five-run matrix removes the strongest single-resolution and small-domain
objections to the eval-118 shortcut. Its peak remains between $17.27\%$ and
$19.63\%$, the fine--medium discrepancy is modest, and the domain dependence
is below one percent. Constraint norms remain bounded and every geodesic trace
passes the ray-completion and null-Hamiltonian gates.

Three limitations define the claim. First, the matter is predominantly exotic,
so the construction does not provide a conventional material source. Second,
confinement falls to roughly one quarter by $t=30$; the geometry is a transient
precursor, not a stable bubble. Third, the resolution sequence is not
monotonic. Agreement under refinement is useful evidence, but it is not a
continuum extrapolation. The preliminary $\Psi_4$ samples likewise do not yet
establish a gravitational-wave spectrum.

\section{Conclusion}

A matter-first MAP-Elites search identified eval 118, a five-lump exotic
complex-scalar shell producing an evolving null-geodesic shortcut. Production
validation gives $f_{\rm geo}=17.38\%$ in the baseline run and
$17.27$--$19.63\%$ over five resolution/domain tests, with all rays completing
and acceptable null-Hamiltonian drift. The effect is numerically robust over
the tested matrix but not Richardson-convergent, and the supporting matter
disperses. The result is a reproducible transient spacetime shortcut, not a
functional warp drive.
