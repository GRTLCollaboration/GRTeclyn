\begin{abstract}
We present an automated, \emph{matter-first} search for faster-than-light (FTL)
precursor spacetimes that are genuine solutions of the Einstein equations rather
than hand-designed metrics.  A quality-diversity optimizer proposes
configurations of \emph{sub-luminal} scalar matter; \texttt{GRTresna} solves the
elliptic Hamiltonian and momentum constraints for the geometry that matter
actually sources; \texttt{GRTeclyn} evolves the result in full $3{+}1$ general
relativity on GPU; and gauge-invariant null-geodesic, operational, and
energy-condition probes score each candidate.  The search runs in three stages:
broad MAP-Elites illumination, CMA-ES refinement under a robustness objective,
and high-resolution replay for validation.  The pipeline is constraint-clean by
construction---each surviving candidate passes a post-load constraint gate under
the same stress-energy that is evolved---so a candidate is a self-consistent
spacetime, not a state that violates $G_{ab}=8\pi T_{ab}$ on load.  Our headline
result, candidate \texttt{eval\,177}, is a constraint-satisfying, non-collapsing
spacetime, sourced by five sub-luminal scalar clouds, that develops a
reliability-gated, gauge-invariant null-geodesic shortcut of
$f_{\rm geo}\!\approx\!5.9\%$ ($\approx\!1.06c$ effective relative to the
asymptotic rest frame), \emph{sustained for $\sim\!16\,M$} before relaxing,
with a total exotic-matter content roughly an order of magnitude below a
comparable Alcubierre bubble evaluated on the identical measure.  We stress what
the result is \emph{not}: the shortcut is transient and exotic-matter supported,
and no passenger region is transported---it is a warp \emph{precursor}, not a
drive.  We validate the probes against a prescribed Alcubierre metric (recovered
$\sim\!32\%$ shortcut, flagged exotic matter) and a flat control, and we outline
the concrete path---a persistence/transport objective, an exotic-energy
feasibility frontier, non-dispersing (boson-star) matter, and a full
observer-robust, resolution-converged, analytically-extracted validation
ladder---that turns the discovery engine into a falsifiable physics program.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Warp and wormhole spacetimes are almost universally studied by
\emph{prescribing the metric}: one writes down a target metric
(Alcubierre~\cite{helmerich2024warpfactory}, Nat\'ario, \ldots) and reads off the
stress-energy $T_{ab}=G_{ab}/8\pi$ it requires.  That matter is invariably exotic, is never obtained as the solution of
a consistent initial-value problem, and the ``FTL'' is built into the chosen
coordinates---it need not survive a real evolution.  Throughout this paper
``FTL'' denotes an \emph{effective} shortcut through curved spacetime relative to
a flat control: a null signal that traverses the region faster than light could
between the same asymptotic endpoints in vacuum.  It never implies local
superluminal motion outside the light cone; in all candidates considered here
both the matter and any test ray remain strictly sub-luminal locally.

This work inverts the construction.  The optimizer proposes \emph{matter}; an
elliptic solver returns the compatible geometry; that geometry is evolved; and
probes measure whether an FTL signature emerges and \emph{persists}.  Any signal
that survives this loop is a property of a self-consistent, evolved spacetime.
The scientific question is operational and falsifiable: \emph{which distributions
of ordinary, sub-luminal matter, if any, source a real (gauge-invariant) null
shortcut, how long does it persist, and at what exotic-energy cost?}

The contribution of this paper is threefold.  (i)~A validated, end-to-end
matter-first discovery pipeline (Sec.~\ref{sec:pipeline}) with positive and
negative probe controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:probes}).  (ii)~A concrete, reproducible
self-consistent candidate, \texttt{eval\,177}, analyzed in detail at high
resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), together with an apples-to-apples
exotic-matter comparison against an Alcubierre bubble.  (iii)~An honest appraisal
of what has and has not been achieved, and a prioritized roadmap toward
persistent and transport-capable candidates (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).  We make
no claim of transport or of a warp drive; we report a self-consistent warp
\emph{precursor} and the machinery that found it.

\section{Matter-first pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\subsection{Search loop and objective}
\label{sec:loop}

The optimizer sees only a scalar objective; physics enters through an
initial-data map and through post-evolution diagnostics,
\[
\theta \;\rightarrow\; \text{initial data}
\;\rightarrow\; \texttt{GRTeclyn}
\;\rightarrow\; \text{diagnostics}
\;\rightarrow\; S(\theta).
\]
For scalar matter the constraint sources are
\begin{equation}
\rho=\tfrac12\psi^{-4}|\tilde\nabla\phi|^2+\tfrac12\Pi^2+V(\phi),
\qquad
S_i=-\Pi\,\partial_i\phi ,
\label{eq:scalar_sources}
\end{equation}
and \texttt{GRTresna} solves the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints for the
conformal factor and longitudinal extrinsic curvature, supplying the
momentum-carrying matter that a prescribed-metric ansatz cannot supply.

The score combines four classes of diagnostics: shortcut indicators, the
operational and gauge-invariant FTL probes (Sec.~\ref{sec:probes}), health terms
(survival, constraint norms, lapse collapse, trapped-surface flags), and risk
terms (required negative energy, matter and effective energy conditions,
tidal/curvature proxies).  A non-triviality gate suppresses flat space, so the
optimizer cannot win by selecting ``nothing happens.''  The exact weights are
not essential here; the operative requirement is that a candidate be
simultaneously a \emph{real shortcut} and a \emph{healthy, constraint-satisfying,
evolved spacetime}.

\subsection{Shell parameterization}
\label{sec:shell}

The discovery ansatz is a low-dimensional ``shell'' vector that expands into a
small number of scalar clouds placed on a Fibonacci lattice over the sphere,
rotated by a searched orientation axis, and assigned toroidal, poloidal, and
radial velocities.  Low-order dipole/quadrupole modulations control matter
asymmetry; an exotic wedge selects where negative-energy support is attempted; a
scalar mass and quartic coupling are searchable, and a static toggle can zero all
matter currents.  MAP-Elites is the natural top-level optimizer because
preserving diverse \emph{mechanism families} by behavior cell is more valuable
than collapsing to a single scalar optimum.

\subsection{Self-consistency: matched matter and the post-load gate}
\label{sec:selfconsistent}

\texttt{GRTresna} convergence is necessary but not sufficient: the source it
solves must be the stress-energy \texttt{GRTeclyn} actually evolves after loading.
We enforce this with three measures.  (i)~\texttt{GRTeclyn} evolves a dedicated
independent signed-scalar matter model that mirrors the solver source---each lump
carries its own sign, kinetic and momentum terms are summed per lump, and no
spurious inter-lump cross terms are introduced.  (ii)~The evolution potential is
the mass-matched form $V(\phi)=\tfrac12 m^2\phi^2$ using the solver mass.
(iii)~A \emph{post-load constraint gate}: after the \texttt{.gridinit} is written,
it is loaded into \texttt{GRTeclyn}, the Hamiltonian and momentum residuals are
evaluated with the evolution matter model, and candidates with $\|H\|_{L^2}$ or
$\|M\|_{L^2}$ above $10^{-2}$ are rejected before GPU evolution.  Exotic
(negative-energy) configurations additionally use a $K=0$ maximal-slicing solve
to avoid the imaginary-$K$ ansatz where $\rho<0$.  With these in place a
surviving candidate is a genuine solution of $G_{ab}=8\pi T_{ab}$, not an
artifact that violates the field equations on load; representative post-load
residuals are $\|H\|_{L^2}\!\approx\!4\times10^{-3}$,
$\|M\|_{L^2}\!\approx\!2\times10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Three-stage search}
\label{sec:stages}

The campaign proceeds in three stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).  \emph{Stage~1
(MAP-Elites QD)} illuminates an $8\times8$ behavior archive at survey resolution
($N=128$, two refinement levels, $t=16$) over $\sim\!10^3$ evaluations, keeping
the best candidate per behavior cell plus an FTL ``hall of fame'' so that high
mid-run shortcut peaks are never pruned for a poor end-state score.  \emph{Stage~2
(CMA-ES)} performs local refinement around the most physically robust QD
survivors under a \texttt{robust\_ftl} objective that rewards a persistent,
healthy, low-exotic gauge-invariant shortcut rather than a flashy transient.
\emph{Stage~3 (high-resolution replay)} re-runs the best candidate at validation
resolution ($N=256$, three refinement levels, $t=30$, plot cadence
$\Delta t\!\approx\!0.24$) with the full probe suite and incremental, per-frame
scoring.  Stages~1--2 are the discovery engine; Stage~3 is the falsification
test.

\section{Diagnostics and probe validation}
\label{sec:probes}

\subsection{Operational and gauge-invariant FTL}

The coordinate (operational) probe compares the fastest causal coordinate-time
path across the slice to a flat baseline between the same endpoints,
\begin{equation}
F_{\rm op}=\frac{t_B^{\rm flat}-t_B^{\rm curved}}{t_B^{\rm flat}},
\label{eq:f_op}
\end{equation}
evaluated by a Dijkstra search over the exact one-way coordinate light speed
obtained from the full $3{+}1$ data $(\alpha,\beta^i,\gamma_{ij})$, so it detects
a shortcut of any mechanism or orientation.  $F_{\rm op}$ is, however, partly
gauge dependent.  The arbiter is the \emph{gauge-invariant} null-geodesic probe:
we integrate the null geodesic equation in Hamiltonian form,
$\dot x^\mu=g^{\mu\nu}k_\nu$, $\dot k_\mu=-\tfrac12(\partial_\mu g^{\alpha\beta})
k_\alpha k_\beta$, on the evolved metric and define the fractional travel-time
saving between two asymptotic static observers in the flat far field,
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm geo}=\max\!\left(0,\;\frac{t_{\rm flat}-t_{\rm min}}{t_{\rm flat}}\right),
\label{eq:f_geo}
\end{equation}
which is gauge invariant because the endpoints lie in the Minkowski asymptotic
region.  Each ray bundle is reliability-gated on the null constraint
$H=\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu$ (relative drift below $10^{-2}$), with a
higher-resolution re-trace to certify sharp-walled shortcuts.  The corresponding
effective signal speed between the distant observers is $c/(1-f_{\rm geo})$.  The
required (exotic) energy density tracked on the risk side is
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\rm req}=\frac{R+K^2-K_{ij}K^{ij}}{16\pi}.
\label{eq:rho_req}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exotic-energy budget}
\label{sec:exotic_measure}

To quantify ``how much exotic matter'' on a single, transferable measure we
integrate the Eulerian energy density $\rho=T_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu$ (with $n$ the
unit normal to the slice) over its negative part with the proper volume element,
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm exotic}=\left|\int_{\rho<0}\rho\,\sqrt{\det\gamma}\;d^3x\right|,
\label{eq:eexotic}
\end{equation}
in geometric units.  Because $\rho=\rho_{\rm req}$ via the Hamiltonian
constraint, Eq.~\eqref{eq:eexotic} is computed identically for an analytic metric
and for an evolved candidate, making the comparison in
Sec.~\ref{sec:exotic_results} apples-to-apples.

\subsection{Probe controls}
\label{sec:controls}

The probes are calibrated against two analytic controls evaluated by the same
code.  A prescribed Alcubierre bubble ($v_s=2$, radius $4$, wall steepness $2$)
yields a gauge-invariant shortcut $f_{\rm geo}\!\approx\!0.32$ and a clearly
flagged exotic, null-energy-condition--violating stress-energy; flat Minkowski
space returns $f_{\rm geo}=0$ and no exotic content to numerical precision.  The
probes therefore light up where a real shortcut exists and stay dark where none
does, so a null gauge-invariant reading on a candidate is informative rather than
a blind spot.

\section{Results: a self-consistent warp precursor}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Provenance}

Candidate \texttt{eval\,177} is the Stage~2 (CMA-ES, \texttt{robust\_ftl})
winner, seeded from the most robust Stage~1 survivors and refined over $200$
evaluations; its objective improved monotonically from $\sim\!213$ to $312$.  Its
matter is five sub-luminal scalar clouds (coordinate speeds
$\sim\!0.2$--$0.8\,c$) with scalar mass $m\!\approx\!1.25$, a quartic coupling,
and a high exotic fraction.  It is the only promoted candidate whose lumps carry
genuine momentum (the others reduce to static lenses), and the only one that
completes the $t=30$ high-resolution replay without forming a trapped surface.
All quoted numbers below are from the Stage~3 high-resolution run
($N=256$, three refinement levels, $t=30$, $126$ frames).

\subsection{What is moving faster than light}
\label{sec:whatmoves}

Nothing material, and no local light ray, exceeds $c$.  The scalar clouds are
sub-luminal and only \emph{source} the geometry.  The FTL signature is a
transient warp \emph{channel for light}: a null signal routed through the region
arrives $\approx\!5.9\%$ early relative to the asymptotic rest frame, i.e.\ as if
it had averaged $\approx\!1.06c$ over the crossing, while every photon travels at
exactly $c$ on its local, geometry-tilted cone.  This is the same mechanism that
makes an Alcubierre bubble ``FTL,'' grown self-consistently from ordinary matter
instead of prescribed.  A measurement caveat applies and is stated plainly: the
geodesic probe ray-traces each \emph{frozen} snapshot, whereas the geometry
evolves on $\sim\!10\,M$, comparable to the $\sim\!16\,M$ light-crossing time, so
the true end-to-end shortcut for a ray threading the evolving channel requires a
$3{+}1$ ray trace and may be smaller than the snapshot peak.

\label{sec:exotic_results}

$\sim\!4.6\times$ less at the moment the warp is active), and its pointwise

qualification is that this is largely because \texttt{eval\,177} is a far gentler,

self-consistency---\texttt{eval\,177} is an evolved solution sourced by
sub-luminal matter, whereas the Alcubierre datum is a hand-built metric that does
not satisfy a solved initial-value problem.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation and limitations}

\texttt{eval\,177} establishes that an automated matter-first search can produce a
\emph{self-consistent}, constraint-clean, non-collapsing spacetime, sourced by
ordinary sub-luminal matter, that carries a reliable, gauge-invariant null
shortcut for a macroscopic fraction of the evolution.  That a real shortcut forms
\emph{and persists for} $\sim\!16\,M$ in a solved-and-evolved spacetime---rather
than appearing only in $t=0$ initial data and dying on the first step---is the
substantive finding.

We are equally explicit about what it is not.  (i)~It is a warp
\emph{precursor}, not transport: the structure stands and relaxes; no localized
passenger region is carried a finite distance.  (ii)~The shortcut is
\emph{transient}: it forms, peaks, and dissipates rather than holding stationary.
(iii)~It requires \emph{exotic matter}; the null energy condition is violated, as
for every known warp shortcut.  (iv)~The headline $f_{\rm geo}$ is measured on
frozen snapshots (Sec.~\ref{sec:whatmoves}); the through-the-evolving-channel
value is not yet established.  None of these are presented as solved; they define
the work that remains.

\subsection{Toward persistent and transport-capable candidates}
\label{sec:roadmap}

The single most consequential change is to \emph{score the spacetime object, not
a slice}.  The present objective rewards a shortcut on a time slice and therefore
favors a transient, pulsing lens.  We will replace it with a world-tube
functional split into two well-posed targets: a \emph{persistent standing
channel}---reward $f_{\rm geo}$ sustained above threshold while the comoving
metric is approximately stationary ($\partial_t$ small), which converts
\texttt{eval\,177}'s decaying pulse into a traversable standing shortcut---and a
\emph{transport world-tube}---reward a localized, approximately flat passenger
region that moves a finite proper distance with bounded tides, the only condition
that earns the word ``drive.''

We further propose: (i)~an \emph{exotic-energy feasibility frontier}, mapping the
Pareto trade-off between shortcut, persistence, and the negative-energy integral
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:eexotic}], which is a quantitative result tied to averaged-null
and quantum-inequality bounds even in the absence of a drive; (ii)~a
\emph{non-dispersing matter model}---a complex (boson-star) scalar with a
conserved charge whose stationary profile directly attacks the persistence
limitation that dispersing real-scalar clouds impose; (iii)~stronger search
machinery---a CMA-ES--driven archive illuminator (CMA-ME) and a learned
feasibility surrogate to suppress the substantial fraction of proposals rejected
by the constraint solve; and (iv)~completion of the validation ladder for any
surviving candidate: a full $3{+}1$ null-ray trace through the evolving metric
(retiring the frozen-snapshot caveat), an observer-robust energy-condition audit,
a resolution-convergence study, and \emph{analytic extraction}---fitting a
closed-form metric/matter profile, re-solving the constraints from that form, and
replaying it---which converts a numerical coefficient vector into a checkable,
citable spacetime.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have demonstrated an automated, matter-first discovery pipeline for dynamical
spacetimes and used it to produce a self-consistent, constraint-clean,
non-collapsing solution---candidate \texttt{eval\,177}---in which ordinary
sub-luminal scalar matter sources a reliability-gated, gauge-invariant null
shortcut of $\approx\!6\%$ ($\approx\!1.06c$ effective) sustained for
$\sim\!16\,M$, at roughly an order of magnitude lower exotic-matter content than a
comparable Alcubierre bubble on the identical measure.  The probes are validated
against analytic positive and negative controls, and the result is reported with
its limitations foregrounded: it is a transient, exotic-supported warp
\emph{precursor}, not transport.  The value of the work is methodological and
falsifiable---a discovery engine that honestly rejects its own elites---and the
path forward is concrete: a persistence/transport objective, an exotic-energy
